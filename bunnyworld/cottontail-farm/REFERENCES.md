# Cottontail Farm: references for the one-idea pass (2026-11-16)

Why: an outside visitor called Cottontail "overdesigned", and the critic (notes/bunny-world/critic/REVIEW.md, sections B2, B3, C1, D4, E and G) found the cleverest feature on the street shown in its dead state, with eight features and no opening line. The pass opens on one idea: the season that's on, or the festival when nothing is on.

Borrowed: structure and restraint only. Nothing of their look, colour, type or branding is used.

| Reference (Refero) | What its first screen does | The one trait borrowed |
|---|---|---|
| PlayStation The Concert, playstation.com/en-us/theconcert ([Refero](https://refero.design/pages/e7672065-e676-4901-8621-2b2992055155)) | One claim over one big photo and a single "Get tickets"; tour dates, gallery and FAQ wait further down | One headline, one photo, one ticket button; everything else moves below the first screen |
| SeatGeek event page, Wonderfront Festival ([Refero](https://refero.design/pages/f17b2c9c-fa80-429d-a8de-bbd04a17ab88)) | The event name, then when and where in one line, then one ticket action; when nothing is on sale it says so plainly and offers one "Track" button | The date line under the headline, and off sale shown as one plain sentence with one sign-up instead of a wall of "closed" |
| Volkshotel agenda event ([Refero](https://refero.design/pages/5198b4fb-3ab7-4cd7-b1bc-f52e99b9a837)) | A poster-like hero for one event; the logistics sit in a side block lower down | The hero is about one event (the festival), and logistics (hours, map, groups) come after it |
| Cake Equity event page ([Refero](https://refero.design/pages/6532f5da-09ed-4e26-91f3-57556966cc81)) | A flat colour field holding the title, the date line and one button, beside one photo | A flat season-colour field with the copy beside the photo, no overlays or gradients |

## What was cut or folded

- The weather line and the "See a closure day" button in the top bar.
- The season tabs in the hero; the other seasons are now one line near the bottom ("See it in summer", "See it in the off-season"). It's a demo control, not something a farm visitor uses.
- The year timeline (Jan to Dec ticks) and the "What's open now" list (the map list already carries it).
- The days-to-go counter beside the button.
- The header "Get tickets" button that repeated the hero button.
- Three of the four email sign-ups. The one left is the off-season ticket form ("Tell me when").
- The hiring block is one line that opens the roles and the form, shown in season only.
- Clipped job lines ("Flags, smiles, sunscreen.") rewritten as full sentences.

The default view: in season the page opens on what's on (sunflowers in August, the festival from September 1). Out of season it opens on the festival, and the line near the bottom says plainly that it's the off-season at the farm right now.
